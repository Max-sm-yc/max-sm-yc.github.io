A Civilization of Protagonists

Max Sun·Essay

AI has the potential to untether human labor from economic prosperity. Today, civilization is constrained by the availability of human labor, and most people spend their lives working to sustain the economic system. Automated production could lift this constraint.

However, this does not mean that humans will be relegated to irrelevance. In fact, this transformation will allow for a new society, a civilization of protagonists. Our current society cannot economically sustain billions of people pursuing resource-intensive and ambitious undertakings; many must work in economically productive but undesirable occupations to enable others to pursue more desirable ones. In a civilization of automated production, we may find ourselves, not just with enough abundance to provide an all-encompassing welfare state, but also to support entire populations living out extraordinary lives. Imagine a world where anyone has the resources to explore another planet, pursue large research initiatives, and invent new technologies they fancy.

This would be a machine empire with a human heart. Each person could command productive resources available to only a select few in our current society, perhaps equivalent to the labor of hundreds of thousands of human workers.

It would be an unprecedented democratization of human ambition. Today, ambition is constrained because the ability to enact change often requires commanding the labor of others. When labor is not human, power to enact change no longer implies power over other humans, removing the implicitly selective nature of certain ambitions and making them available for all.

This wouldn’t mean that every person will become an explorer, scientist, entrepreneur, or artist. Ambition should remain a choice, not an expectation. Certainly, no one should be ashamed in any society to seek a peaceful existence for themselves and their family. But for those who would gaze at the stars, they could think:

“why not me?”
